    ## Overview

    This document outlines the hardware conventions used in the Quantum Universal
    Application Module (QUAM). It serves as a reference for engineers and researchers
    working with QUAM hardware systems.


    ## Hardware Naming Conventions

    1. **Qubit Identifiers**: Qubits are identified using the format `Qx`, where `x`
    is a unique integer (e.g., Q1, Q2, Q3).

    2. **Control Lines**: Control lines are labeled as `CLx`, where `x` corresponds
    to the qubit number they control.

    3. **Readout Lines**: Readout lines are labeled as `RLx`, where `x` corresponds
    to the qubit number they read.


    ## Signal Standards

    1. **Frequency Range**: All signals must operate within the 4-8 GHz range.

    2. **Power Levels**: Signal power levels should not exceed -20 dBm at the qubit
    interface.

    3. **Impedance Matching**: All lines must be impedance-matched to 50 ohms.


    ## Hardware Safety Guidelines

    1. **Temperature Control**: Ensure the dilution refrigerator is operating at 10
    mK or lower.

    2. **Signal Isolation**: Use isolators and circulators to prevent back-reflection
    and cross-talk.

    3. **Grounding**: All equipment must be properly grounded to avoid electrical
    hazards.


    ## Maintenance Schedule

    1. **Weekly**: Inspect all control and readout lines for wear and tear.

    2. **Monthly**: Calibrate signal generators and analyzers.

    3. **Quarterly**: Perform a full system diagnostic to ensure optimal performance.


    ## Troubleshooting Common Issues

    1. **Signal Loss**: Check for loose connections or damaged cables.

    2. **Frequency Drift**: Recalibrate the signal generator.

    3. **Qubit Decoherence**: Verify temperature stability and signal isolation.


    ## Documentation and Reporting

    All maintenance and troubleshooting activities must be documented in the system
    log. Any deviations from standard operating procedures should be reported to the
    hardware team lead immediately.'

## Overview
This document outlines the hardware conventions used in the Quantum Universal Application Module (QUAM). It serves as a reference for engineers and researchers working with QUAM hardware systems.

## Hardware Naming Conventions
1. **Qubit Identifiers**: Qubits are identified using the format `Qx`, where `x` is a unique integer (e.g., Q1, Q2, Q3).
2. **Control Lines**: Control lines are labeled as `CLx`, where `x` corresponds to the qubit number they control.
3. **Readout Lines**: Readout lines are labeled as `RLx`, where `x` corresponds to the qubit number they read.

## Signal Standards
1. **Frequency Range**: All signals must operate within the 4-8 GHz range.
2. **Power Levels**: Signal power levels should not exceed -20 dBm at the qubit interface.
3. **Impedance Matching**: All lines must be impedance-matched to 50 ohms.

## Hardware Safety Guidelines
1. **Temperature Control**: Ensure the dilution refrigerator is operating at 10 mK or lower.
2. **Signal Isolation**: Use isolators and circulators to prevent back-reflection and cross-talk.
3. **Grounding**: All equipment must be properly grounded to avoid electrical hazards.

## Maintenance Schedule
1. **Weekly**: Inspect all control and readout lines for wear and tear.
2. **Monthly**: Calibrate signal generators and analyzers.
3. **Quarterly**: Perform a full system diagnostic to ensure optimal performance.

## Troubleshooting Common Issues
1. **Signal Loss**: Check for loose connections or damaged cables.
2. **Frequency Drift**: Recalibrate the signal generator.
3. **Qubit Decoherence**: Verify temperature stability and signal isolation.

## Documentation and Reporting
All maintenance and troubleshooting activities must be documented in the system log. Any deviations from standard operating procedures should be reported to the hardware team lead immediately.
